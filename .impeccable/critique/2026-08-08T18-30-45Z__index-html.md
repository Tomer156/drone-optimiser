## Design Specificity Verdict

Authored for this product, with a borrowed outer shell. The instrument is specific; the chrome around it is not.

Product-specific: the Required-vs-selected table as the central rhetorical device; the sweet-spot band pinned to the throttle axis in userSpaceOnUse so the green stays on 40-60% wherever the curve starts; the frame schematic with non-scaling strokes and HTML dimension overlays; the Calc chain as a lab artefact; mono for measured values and sans for words, without exception; the mass donut using a grey ramp because hue is reserved for fit.

Category-interchangeable: the toolbar, the numbered wizard rail, all three modals, and the two chart headers, which would sit unchanged in any analytics product. The ink promo card and the ink Coming soon slab are marketing furniture and are the two highest-contrast objects on their screens.

Deterministic scan: 42 CLI findings, exit 2. Ten sit inside the vendored runtime (self-labelled GENERATED, lines 2786-4704) and are false positives. Georgia is flagged but DESIGN.md declares it as the notation face, so that gap is in the token file rather than the code. The 14 overused-font hits are one documented decision, not fourteen. Real: 12 authored radius violations against a three-value spec, three colour literals, two layout transitions. Browser overlay: 1,197 elements injected, 589 findings, of which 578 undersized-ui-text collapse to 33 visible, all exactly 10 px, which is a documented step in the ramp.

## Overall Impression

The physics and the prose are the best things here and they are genuinely uncommon. The failure is that the interface does not act on what it knows. It computes a critical fault and files it on a tab the user has no reason to open. It computes a 9.9 Ah shortfall and renders it as an unlabelled red number. It computes a build, and then lets a completed background sweep overwrite the user's explicit Reset. The single biggest opportunity is to make the app say one sentence about what it found.

## What's Working

1. The tooltip corpus. 22 hints, plain English, none containing a formula, several parameterised with the user's own numbers. Hand-written, not generated.
2. The Calc chain. Parameter, symbol, formula, prose and value across 22 derivation rows. It converts a black box into an auditable instrument, and nothing else in this category ships one.
3. Measured discipline. Nine font sizes with zero half-steps, zero WCAG contrast failures across four state-by-theme combinations, and focus indicators at 17.8:1 on 15 of 15 elements tested.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Reset does not cancel the running sweep, and the sweep overwrites the reset**
Why it matters: a destructive action, gated behind a dialog promising "There is no undo", is then undone by the application itself two seconds later, with a motor the user never chose.
Fix: carry an epoch counter, increment it in reset() and in every input handler, and bail in chooseBuild if it has changed. Disable Reset during the sweep, or make it abort the sweep.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] The one critical finding is visible only on a tab the user has no reason to open**
Why it matters: the highest-severity output of the tool is hidden while the default tab actively reassures with "1 to review".
Fix: compute maximum severity across all tabs and surface it on the Results badge; add a severity dot to the Electronics tab label.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P1] Pro's front door is 317 unsearchable rows opening on twelve rejects**
Why it matters: this is the paid interaction and it is hostile on first contact. The motor driving the current results sits 11,583 px below the fold.
Fix: scroll the selection into view and mark it; default the filter to Fits; sort by fit then by the active goal; add a text filter; keep the spec line visible when a row fails.
Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P1] System efficiency shows a number that is not the number it produces, and its no-op is destructive**
Why it matters: the slider reads 90%, the note beneath reads 71%, and the flags card reads 75/100/95. Dragging to 91 and back to 90 changes the model to 73% with no route back except a full Reset.
Fix: label the slider with the figure it produces, or drive the three terms proportionally from their defaults. A round trip to the starting position must be a no-op.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] The no-scroll rule holds only at 1440x900 and above**
Why it matters: at 1024x768 the results pane scrolls 380 px; at 1280x700 the rail hides the entire Optimise-for section including the button; at 375 px six toolbar controls sit off-screen and fail hit-testing with no horizontal scroll.
Fix: collapse the rail's sections below a height threshold, reflow the toolbar, and decide explicitly whether below 1024 is supported.
Suggested command: /impeccable adapt

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user)**: 317 rows, no search, no sort, selection not scrolled into view. No way to compare two motors or two props. No build history, and Back is not wired to state. Pro's last step offers only Back, so he cannot say "given the prop I picked, find me the best pack". Credibility break: a 140C sticker printed as 910 A with a 34.06x margin, in the same visual register as 1,761 rows of bench data.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent)**: zero focusable elements in the entire results area. 16 of 22 tooltip hosts are non-focusable divs. Ten range inputs render 247x4 px against WCAG 2.5.8's 24x24. Zero aria-selected, zero aria-expanded, and role=tablist contains zero role=tab. Three modals that never move, trap or return focus and ignore Escape. The live region announces "Build ready: 7 minutes" for a build that misses a 22-minute target. The only confirmation that Tooltips were turned off is a tooltip.

**Riley (stress tester)**: reset mid-sweep resurrects the build, deterministically. The efficiency slider round trip changes the answer irreversibly. 375 px overprints 58 text pairs. 1299x910 clips flag copy. Badge arithmetic is unstable across states. The slider floor yields "12% of pack energy reaches the props" and the app reports the resulting flight time as fact.

## Questions to Consider

1. Are "22 min" and "8.02 g/W" genuinely the same class of object, or has efficiency been promoted to display size for symmetry rather than importance?
2. What would it cost to mark marketing-derived numbers differently from measured ones, and would that make the measured ones worth more?
3. If Pro is the product, why does its front door open on twelve motors that cannot lift the drone?
4. This screen contains no sentence. For a tool that decides whether an aircraft will fly, what is lost by writing one line of prose that says what happened?
5. There is no end state. What is the user meant to walk away holding, and should it be a parts list they can order?
